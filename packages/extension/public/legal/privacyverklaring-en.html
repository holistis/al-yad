How we handle page content (DOM) in these logs — a concrete, verifiable commitment. Our logs under the controller role are intended for the operation, security and diagnosis of the Service, not for recording the content of the automated pages. Therefore:

  • a) By default we filter page content (DOM text, form values, screenshots) out of our technical, usage and security logs. Our logging records metadata (such as the type of action, time, status, error code), not the content of the fields on the page.
  • b) If, in the event of a malfunction or security incident, a fragment of page content should nonetheless incidentally end up in an error message or diagnostic log, then we treat that fragment as data under the processor role, apply pseudonymisation/redaction where technically possible, strictly limit access and retention (see 7.1), and use it solely to resolve the malfunction or the incident — never for a purpose of our own.
  • c) Insofar as, despite (a) and (b), personal data of third parties should unexpectedly end up in our own logs, we rely on our legitimate interest in a safe and reliable Service (4.3/4.4) and on the exception of Article 14(5)(b) GDPR (disproportionate effort to inform each third party individually), whereby this statement and the information that the Customer provides to its data subjects together safeguard transparency. We actively minimise this residual processing and erase such fragments as quickly as operationally possible.

Here we deliberately use no non-committal wording such as "we strive to". The commitment under (a) is an implemented filtering; the honestly stated residual processing under (b)/(c) is the part that cannot be 100% technically excluded, and we would rather acknowledge that than conceal it.

5. Our role: processor versus controller

5.1 Yad has a hybrid role:

  • Controller for its own business operations: account, invoicing, telemetry/log and security data and own marketing (Chapter 4). For these processing operations we determine the purpose and means, and this statement applies in full.
  • Processor for carrying out automation tasks on the instruction of the Customer (Chapter 3.4). Here we process personal data solely on the documented instruction of the Customer, who is controller for this data.

5.2 For the processor role we conclude with every Customer a data processing agreement (DPA) on the basis of Article 28 GDPR. This states, among other things: the binding to instructions, confidentiality, security measures, the regime for sub-processors, assistance with requests from data subjects and data breaches, and return or erasure after the end of the agreement. This public statement does not replace the DPA; in the event of conflict about the processor role, the DPA prevails.

5.3 Why Yad is processor and not (joint) controller for the session/DOM processing. We have substantiated this allocation of roles and had it established by a lawyer [DATE/REFERENCE role analysis]. The core of that substantiation:

  • a) The Customer determines the purpose. The Customer decides which task is carried out on which system; Yad merely provides the means (the agent) to carry out that task determined by the Customer.
  • b) The technical choices of the agent are means-choices, not purpose-determination. That the agent itself determines which DOM fragments are submitted to the language model for carrying out the task and how pseudonymisation/filtering is done concerns the manner of performance (the "how"), within the instruction given by the Customer and the "why". It does not make Yad the determiner of the purpose.
  • c) We do not use this data for our own purposes (see 5.4 and 4.10). As soon as we were to do so, we would for that part become controller ourselves (Article 28(10) GDPR) — which we exclude.

Honest reservation: whether a supervisory authority or court would in a specific case nonetheless qualify Yad as (joint) controller is a factual and legal assessment that may differ per situation. That is why the role analysis under 5.3 is a mandatory, periodically to-be-recalibrated precondition (Chapter 16), and we inform the Customer's data subjects via the route described below (5.5) instead of relying solely on the Customer.

5.4 Preventing creeping role change — linked to a verifiable measure. We do not use data obtained in our processor role for our own purposes. This is not a loose promise: we secure it with technical separation between the processor environment and our own administration and with audit logging on access to session/page data, so that improper use is traceable. Were we nonetheless to use this data for our own purposes, we would for that part become controller ourselves (Article 28(10) GDPR); that is expressly not our intention and we prevent this in our processes.

5.5 Information to the Customer's data subjects (Article 14 GDPR). For personal data of third parties that the Service touches in the session, the Customer is in principle controller and the Customer must inform those data subjects (Articles 13/14 GDPR). Because Yad has no direct relationship with these data subjects, Yad does not inform them individually; that would require a disproportionate effort (Article 14(5)(b) GDPR). We do make this public statement generally accessible, so that the Customer's data subjects too can ascertain what role Yad plays, and we provide the Customer the legally required assistance in informing and answering requests from those data subjects (see 9.3).

8. Security

8.1 We take appropriate technical and organisational measures to protect personal data (Article 32 GDPR), including:

  • Encryption of data in transit and, where appropriate, at rest;
  • Access restriction according to the principle of least privilege and strict access control on the browser extension and the underlying systems;
  • Separation between customers (multi-tenant isolation), so that data of different Customers remains separated;
