Privacy Statement Yad

Yad is a little helper that carries out recurring work in your browser for you. This page explains what happens with your data. It is short, because little happens, and we would rather write down what actually is than a long text about things we do not do.

This statement describes the browser extension and the program that runs alongside it on your computer. It does not cover the server mode, in which Yad runs on a server of your own; in that case you determine yourself what happens with the data.

The short version

The work happens on your computer. The extension itself makes no connection whatsoever to the internet. What does go out, goes out via the program on your computer, and that is precisely the list below. There is nothing more.

If you want nothing at all to go out

Set the setting YAD_LOKAAL to 1. Then Yad uses only a language model that runs on your own computer, and every provider in the cloud is excluded. Not put at the back but excluded, because a fallback would mean that the promise breaks the moment the local model briefly fails, and then you would not notice it. If it does not succeed locally, the task stops with an error message.

In this mode Yad also refuses to start if the local model in reality runs on another computer. That sounds strict, but otherwise "nothing goes out" would be something that could accidentally become untrue.

Honest about the price of this: local is slower. Count on about fifteen seconds per reasoning step versus less than a second with a model in the cloud. For work that runs at night or during lunch you will not notice that. If you are sitting there waiting for it, you will.

What stays on your computer

These things stay with you and go nowhere:

  • Your settings, including the keys of the language models that you use. These are stored in the extension's storage, in plain text. Anyone who can access your logged-in browser profile can therefore access them.
  • Your saved tasks and the history of earlier commands.
  • A temporary memory of successful actions per site, so that the same work goes faster the next time. That is cleaned up after seven days.
  • A log file of the steps, with the visited addresses and the fetched text.

If you want to throw this away, delete the folder with data of the program and the extension from your browser. Then everything is gone.

Why we are allowed to do this

For carrying out the task that you give, the legal basis is the agreement with you: without this processing Yad cannot do the work. For the recovery memory the legal basis is our legitimate interest in making the product work on sites where it goes wrong, and that is also why it is limited to three pieces of data that are not traceable to you. You can turn it off.

Your rights

You may request which data we hold about you, have it corrected or erased, and object. In practice that is brief: apart from the three streams named above we have nothing of yours, and the recovery memory contains no data that is traceable to you. You can also always lodge a complaint with the Dutch Data Protection Authority (Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens).

Children

Yad is intended for business use and not for children under sixteen.

If something goes wrong

If we discover a data breach, we report it within 48 hours to whoever it affects and, where that is required, to the Dutch Data Protection Authority (Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens). That is stricter than the 72 hours that the law prescribes, because we believe that you simply ought to know quickly.

Changes

If something changes about what is stated above, we adapt this page and raise the version number at the bottom. If it concerns a real change in what goes out, then we also report that in the program itself, because a silent change on a web page is not a notification.
